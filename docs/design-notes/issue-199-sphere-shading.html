Design note · Issue #199

Body shading & Sun halo

Every orbiting body reads as a small sphere lit by the central Sun: a directional highlight on the Sun-facing side, an elliptical day/night terminator, and one translucent shadow wash — the same primitive for a planet, the Moon, a comet head, or Saturn with its rings. Plus a zoom-aware halo behind the Sun and a planet palette leaning toward how the planets actually look.

approved Issue: #199 Slices: 2 Area: src/renderer

Context

Bodies are drawn by renderBody() / renderSaturn() / renderCometBody() in src/renderer/bodies.ts. The Sun is always at (CENTER, CENTER), so the light direction for a body at (x, y) is just (CENTER − x, CENTER − y) — no extra state. Two independent switches, derived from card config, drive the look:

  • ShadeOptions.sphere (display: 2d | 3d) — flat disc vs. the #sphere-3d ball gradient.
  • ShadeOptions.dayNight (shading: true | false) — the astronomical day/night wash, Saturn's ring band, and the Sun halo.

Already in place: the translucent day/night wash, Saturn's clipped ring band, the zoom-aware halo (an updateHalo closure), the ShadeOptions split, the #sphere-3d ball, and the palette nudge. Two small slices below: draw the day/night boundary as an ellipse instead of a flat cut, and drop the #sphere-3d highlight down to plain limb darkening.

The two switches stay orthogonal and their jobs stay separate: display: 3d is shape only — the centred #sphere-3d gradient makes every body read as a ball, with no Sun direction. Everything Sun-dependent lives under shading: true: a translucent wash on the anti-sunward hemisphere and nothing on the lit side. A directional #sphere-3d highlight was explored and dropped — it fought the shading layer and never separated cleanly from "make it look round".

Why no eclipticViewDirection. Every point derived from an orbital angle must pass through polarOffset() so the ±1 ecliptic mirror is applied once (CLAUDE.md, #94). The Sun bearing here is not an orbital angle — it is the screen-space vector between (x, y) and (CENTER, CENTER), both already placed and mirror-consistent. So renderBodyShadow and terminatorShadowPath take no direction enum.

Design

PieceMechanism
Light direction Screen-space sunBearing(x, y) = atan2(CENTER − y, CENTER − x) — no eclipticViewDirection (a delta between two placed points, not an orbital angle; CLAUDE.md #94). null at CENTER (the Sun).
display: 3d body A pre-rendered Lambert sphere sprite, blitted as an <image> and tinted to the body's hue by a per-colour <feColorMatrix> (multiply). Two 128 px grayscale sprites ship inline as data URIs (scripts/gen-sphere-sprites.mjs): SPRITE_SOFT (viewer-lit, high ambient — pure volume) when shading is off; SPRITE_LIT (in-plane light, bright side = local +x, a smoothstep-tightened terminator centred near the diameter) when shading is on, with transform="rotate(sunBearing)" so the lit face points at the Sun — the sprite is the day/night. Raster, so it softens a little on zoom. The Sun stays a flat disc in 3d (a light source, not a lit ball; the halo carries its depth).
Day/night (2d) Flat disc + terminatorShadowPath(x, y, r, TERMINATOR_BOW) — the anti-sunward semicircle plus an elliptical arc (sweep 0) bowed into the dark side. Filled by shadeFill(color) = color-mix(in srgb, color 28%, black) at 0.92 — the same in-hue darkening the 3d sprite's dark side uses, so 2d and 3d dark sides match (only the edge differs: hard arc vs soft penumbra). Falls back to #05070c when no colour is given.
Saturn Core = the 3d sprite (or flat disc in 2d). Rings = two stroked circles at opacity 0.8. Day/night = a body-width <clipPath> rect band across the rings + gap, masked by #saturn-core-cut and filled with the same shadeFill(ringColor), so the shadowed rings and shadowed core read as one shadow. In 2d the core also gets the terminatorShadowPath wash; in 3d the sprite covers it (coreShaded).
Sun halo One static radial-gradient circle, sized to the view and rescaled on pan/zoom by the returned updateHalo closure. No filter, no animation.
Palette planet-data.ts hexes nudged toward real planet tones; brightness and saturation kept for the dark theme.

Slices

Slice 1 — elliptical terminator  SHIPPED

  • svg-utils.ts: add terminatorShadowPath(x, y, r, bow, lightFrom = {CENTER, CENTER}) → one d string for the dark region — from pole p1, the anti-sunward semicircle to p2, then an elliptical arc back (rx = bow·r along the Sun axis, ry = r, x-axis-rotation = φ°) bowed into the dark side. Poles at φ ± 90°. Returns null at the light source.
  • bodies.ts: add TERMINATOR_BOW = 0.22 beside SHADOW_FILL / SHADOW_OPACITY. renderBodyShadow's reach === coreR branch calls terminatorShadowPath(x, y, coreR, TERMINATOR_BOW) in place of sunwardHalfDiscPaths(…).darkD; same fill + opacity. Comet heads inherit it. Saturn (reach > coreR) also draws that path on its core disc, and its rect band is now masked by #saturn-core-cut (a white cover minus a coreR circle) so the core is not double-washed.
  • Red test: renderBodyShadow emits a path whose d has two A commands and whose terminator-arc midpoint sits ≈ bow·r off the pole diameter, into the dark side.
  • Touches: new describe("terminatorShadowPath") in svg-utils.test.ts; the two renderBodyShadow / comet-head assertions that compare against sunwardHalfDiscPaths(…).darkD switch to terminatorShadowPath(…).
  • As shipped: arc sweep flag is 0 on the terminator arc (an early 1 bowed it toward the Sun — caught by numeric arc sampling, not the reconstruction-heuristic test). sunwardHalfDiscPaths is now unused by src/. Saturn's core disc gained the terminator + a #saturn-core-cut mask on its ring band (folded in at the review gate). 1243 tests, coverage 100%.
What `display: 3d` went through. An SVG #sphere-3d gradient was tried many ways — centred bright core (read as top-lit), Sun-directional offset focal + rotate-<g> (rotation verified exact to 0.00°, but every stop tune read as a hot spot or a wash), limb-darkening only (read as flat, or blurry at the edge), an opaque in-hue radial fill (still "lit"). The translucent-overlay family can't separate "looks round" from "looks lit". The answer that landed is a pre-baked Lambert sprite — a real shaded sphere, computed once off-line, so there is no runtime <canvas> (which jsdom lacks) and it works as plain SVG <image> + <feColorMatrix>.

Slice 2 — display: 3d = pre-baked Lambert sphere sprite  SHIPPED

  • scripts/gen-sphere-sprites.mjs — a standalone node script (its own minimal PNG encoder, no deps) that renders two 128 px grayscale+alpha spheres: SPRITE_SOFT (L toward the viewer, ambient 0.34) and SPRITE_LIT (L in-plane, bright side +x, ambient 0.28, a lo/hi smoothstep band centred at 0.14 that tightens the terminator without moving it). Both are inlined into bodies.ts as data: URIs (~13 KB total).
  • bodies.ts: renderSphereSprite(svg, x, y, r, color, sunDeg | null) — lazily creates <filter id="tint-hex"> (one feColorMatrix multiplying the grayscale by the hue) and appends <image href=SPRITE_* width=2r filter=... [transform="rotate(sunDeg x y)"]>. sunBearing() exported alongside. renderBody / renderSaturn / renderCometBody call it for shade.sphere (Sun excepted — flat disc); the 2d branch keeps the flat circle + renderBodyShadow.
  • renderBodyShadow gains coreShaded (skip the core wash when the sprite covers it) and shadeColor. New shadeFill(color) helper — color-mix(in srgb, color 28%, black) @ 0.92 — is now used by the 2d wash, the 2d Saturn core, and the Saturn ring band, so every dark side is color × 0.28 whatever the mode.
  • Values dialled on the card via docs/shade-compare.html: sprite L / ambient / lo,hi; SHADE_MIX 28; SHADOW_OPACITY 0.55; ring opacity 0.8; sprite edge feather tightened (rad half, AA in the last 2%) so a 3d body matches a 2d <circle r> within ~1 px.
  • Touches: extensive test-locator rework — image[filter=…] replaces circle[fill=…] for 3d bodies across bodies/comets/index/solar-view tests (bodyCircle/bodyPos/sphere3dCount helpers updated); renderSphere3dDef/renderSphere3d/sphere3dFill deleted; snapshot regenerated. 1244 tests, coverage 100%.

TERMINATOR_BOW = 0.22 and every sprite / shade constant above are provisional values dialled on the real card via /show-it before the slice was accepted.
