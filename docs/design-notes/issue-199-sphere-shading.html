Design note · Issue #199

Body shading, Sun halo & a palette nudge

Light every orbiting body from the central Sun with one translucent shadow layer — the same for a planet, the Moon, a comet head or Saturn with its rings — put a zoom-aware ambient halo behind the Sun, and nudge the planet palette toward how the planets actually look.

approved Issue: #199 Slices: 7 Area: src/renderer

Context

Bodies render as flat discs today: renderBody() in src/renderer/bodies.ts appends a single <circle fill={body.color}>. The Sun is drawn at (CENTER, CENTER) every frame, so the direction to the light for any body at screen position (x, y) is just the vector (CENTER − x, CENTER − y) — no extra state needed. Issue #199 asks for three things built on that: a day/night shadow on every body, a static “energy” halo around the Sun, and a palette revisit (planet-data.ts hexes were picked for contrast on dark slate, not resemblance).

Decisions

QuestionDecision
Shadow mechanismOne translucent dark overlay on the anti-sunward half of a plain disc — same idea as the gallery’s mix-blend-mode tint layer, in SVG. Not an opaque hard half-disc, not a gradient, not stroked arcs.
Applies toEvery body through one primitive: the 8 planets, the Moon, comet heads, and Saturn (body + rings + the gap, as one strip). The Sun gets the halo instead.
Shadow widthThe core planet’s own diameter — a band 2 × coreR from the terminator, running anti-sunward. For a lone body that is exactly its anti-sunward half; for Saturn it is a strip the width of the shrunk body cast across the rings.
ToneSHADOW_FILL = "#05070c" at SHADOW_OPACITY = 0.45. The dark side is body.color under a wash, not an opaque re-fill.
Sun effectStatic radial-gradient halo. No animation, no SVG filter, no prefers-reduced-motion concern.
PaletteSubtle nudge toward realism, brightness/saturation kept. Final hexes signed off against the Preview below.

Preview

Bodies at their real render radii around a central Sun, plus Saturn with rings. Drag the light angle: every terminator and Saturn’s ring strip move together, because all of it is derived from the one Sun–body vector. Toggle flat fills to compare, and the halo on/off.

Illustrative — positions are arbitrary, radii and the proposed palette are real. In the card the light angle isn’t a control; it is fixed by each body’s position relative to screen centre.

The shadow primitive

// bodies.ts
export function renderBodyShadow(
  svg: SVGElement,
  x: number, y: number,
  coreR: number,          // radius of the plain disc already drawn at (x, y)
  reach = coreR,          // how far out the shadow reaches (Saturn: SATURN_RING_OUTER_RADIUS)
): void;                  // no-op when (x, y) === (CENTER, CENTER)  -- the Sun
  • Caller draws the body as a plain <circle fill={color}> first, then calls this.
  • phi = atan2(y − CENTER, x − CENTER) — anti-sunward, measured in screen space between two already-placed points, so it takes no eclipticViewDirection (see callout).
  • reach === coreR — every lone body. The core-width band already covers the whole disc, so the overlay is simply <path d={sunwardHalfDiscPaths(x, y, coreR).darkD} fill={SHADOW_FILL} fill-opacity={SHADOW_OPACITY}>: the anti-sunward half-disc, washed dark. No <clipPath>, no <defs>, no id. sunwardHalfDiscPaths stays as the geometry source; only its darkD is used now.
  • reach > coreR — Saturn only. A <clipPath id="saturn-shadow"> holding one <rect> (2 × coreR tall, reach long, transform="translate(x y) rotate(phiDeg)"), plus a translucent <circle r={reach}> clipped to it — one strip darkening body + both rings + the gap. One fixed id; one Saturn; the SVG is rebuilt every frame.
Why not a clip for every body. A per-body <clipPath> needs a unique id and a <defs> round-trip — ~12× a frame. For a lone body the band is never narrower than the disc, so the clipped result is identical to the plain darkD half-disc path. Take the cheap path there; keep the clip only where it changes the outcome (Saturn’s rings).
Why no eclipticViewDirection. Every point derived from an orbital angle must pass through polarOffset() so the ±1 ecliptic mirror is applied once (CLAUDE.md, #94). phi here is not an orbital angle — it is the screen-space vector between (x, y) and (CENTER, CENTER), both already placed. The Sun stays at CENTER in both views and the body is already mirror-placed, so phi is correct either way. renderBodyShadow and sunwardHalfDiscPaths take no direction enum.

Slice 4 — zoom-aware Sun halo  ACCEPTED

A fixed-radius halo disc read wrong: it wants to be an ambient wash that fills whatever is on screen — the whole system at zoom 1, a tight glow at zoom 4. Zoom is a viewBox swap applied after render (solar-view.ts), so the halo can’t be sized once.

  • renderSolarSystem() gains a fourth return, updateHalo(viewState: PanZoomState) — same pattern as updateMarkers. It sets the halo circle’s r to viewState.width × HALO_VIEW_FRACTION (~0.7 → r 560…224 across the zoom ladder 800/640/480/320). Centred on the Sun at (CENTER, CENTER) always — the glow emanates from the light source, pan doesn’t move it. The objectBoundingBox gradient rescales with the circle for free, so only r changes.
  • renderSunHalo(svg) draws the <radialGradient id="sun-halo"> + <circle id="sun-halo-glow"> once, at the zoom-1 radius (VIEW_SIZE × HALO_VIEW_FRACTION), before the Sun body.
  • solar-view.ts applyViewState(): call updateHalo(panZoomState) next to updateMarkers, right after the viewBox set.
  • Red tests: index.test.ts — renderSolarSystem returns updateHalo; default halo r = 800×fraction; updateHalo({…, width: 320}) sets r = 320×fraction; halo still first (behind the Sun disc). solar-view test — applyViewState calls it.

Shipped: HALO_VIEW_FRACTION = 0.7; halo <circle id="sun-halo-glow" r="560">; updateHalo is the 4th renderSolarSystem return, wired in solar-view.applyViewState() next to updateMarkers. r tracks the live zoom width (smoothly, through the zoom animation). Reviewer: no findings. 1224 tests, 100% coverage.

Retuned (follow-up): the zoom-1 halo read too broad and too bright in the real card. HALO_VIEW_FRACTION 0.7 → 0.33, core stop 0.5 → 0.35, mid stop 55% / 0.14 → 54% / 0.1. Halo tests switched off hard-coded radii onto the HALO_VIEW_FRACTION symbol. docs/halo-tune.html — a live slider harness over the halo gradient — landed with the retune.

Slice 5 — spherical body shading  ACCEPTED

Slices 1–2 landed the shadow as a flat translucent half-disc — deliberately, after the hard-edged terminator read wrong through ~7 geometries (LESSONS.md). Now that the shadow is an overlay wash rather than geometry, the smooth version is cheap: one reusable off-centre <radialGradient> paints a lit sunward face fading to a dark anti-sunward limb — a sphere read, no per-body <defs>. This reverses the Decisions-table “not a gradient” line on purpose: that call was about a hard gradient terminator; this is a soft ambient one.

  • renderSphereShadeDef(svg) — appends one <radialGradient id="sphere-shade"> in objectBoundingBox units with the focal point pushed toward local +x (the lit side): stops from a soft white sunward highlight, through transparent, to #05070c at the anti-sunward limb. Called once from renderSolarSystem, next to renderSunHalo.
  • renderSphereShade(svg, x, y, r) — appends <g transform="rotate(θ x y)"><circle cx cy r fill="url(#sphere-shade)"/></g> where θ = atan2(CENTER − y, CENTER − x) in degrees, so local +x points at the Sun. objectBoundingBox gradient rotates with the group, so one def serves every body. No-op at (CENTER, CENTER).
  • renderBodyShadow — the reach === coreR branch (every lone planet, the Moon, comet heads) now calls renderSphereShade instead of appending the flat darkD <path>. Saturn’s reach > coreR band is untouched this slice — the sphere gradient on its core disc is a follow-up so this slice stays lone-body only.
  • Red tests: bodies.test.ts — off-centre renderBody = plain circle + a <g> whose transform is rotate(…) aimed at CENTER, wrapping a circle[fill="url(#sphere-shade)"]; no flat path[fill="#05070c"] for a lone body; Sun-at-CENTER = no sphere-shade group. index.test.ts — renderSolarSystem emits exactly one #sphere-shade def; body base-disc locators unchanged.

Shipped: renderSphereShadeDef appends one <radialGradient id="sphere-shade" fx="0.72"> (white 0.14 → transparent → #05070c 0.16 → 0.62 at the limb); renderSphereShade(svg, x, y, r) wraps a circle[fill="url(#sphere-shade)"] in <g transform="rotate(θ x y)">, θ = atan2(CENTER − y, CENTER − x) deg — the objectBoundingBox gradient rotates with the group, so one def serves all 9 lone bodies. renderBodyShadow’s reach === coreR branch calls it in place of the flat <path>; the CENTER no-op is now a direct Math.hypot check, dropping bodies.ts’s last sunwardHalfDiscPaths caller. index.ts calls renderSphereShadeDef next to renderSunHalo. Saturn’s band unchanged. 1229 tests, 100% coverage; typecheck + biome green.

Superseded by slice 6: the offset radial gradient put the light/dark midpoint off the disc centre — a dark crescent smaller than half the body, wrong for a top-down view. Slice 6 swaps it for a horizontal linear gradient (terminator at x = 0.5) and extends it to Saturn.

Slice 6 — linear terminator + Saturn  ACCEPTED

An offset radial gradient reads as a small anti-sunward crescent, not a half-lit sphere: its light/dark midpoint sits off the disc centre. A side-lit sphere seen top-down splits exactly in half with a straight terminator through the centre — a linear gradient, not a radial one.

  • renderSphereShadeDef now emits a horizontal <linearGradient id="sphere-shade" x1="0" x2="1">: #05070c 0.6 at x=0 (anti-sunward limb) → ~0.1 at 42% → transparent at 52% (the terminator, disc centre) → #ffffff 0.14 at x=1 (soft sunward highlight). Still objectBoundingBox, still rotated per body by the same <g>, so renderSphereShade is unchanged.
  • renderSaturn gains a trailing renderSphereShade(svg, x, y, coreR) after its ring band — the core disc now reads as a sphere like every other body; the band still crosses the rings.
  • Red tests: bodies.test.ts — linearGradient#sphere-shade with x1/x2, darkest stop first, highlight stop last, a near-transparent stop ~50%; renderSaturn ends with a g > circle[fill="url(#sphere-shade)"] after the band. index.test.ts — the def is a linearGradient; the sphere-overlay count is 10 (Saturn now included).

Shipped: as designed. Q2 answered in-thread — the highlight offset toward the Sun is deliberate; the crescent artefact was the radial gradient, fixed by going linear. 1229 tests, 100% coverage.
