Issue #239 · Design note

AU labels from data, not pixels

approved

Every orbit ring carries a top and bottom “x.x AU” label. renderOrbit() computes them by measuring the drawn ring — pixel distance from the Sun, run back through the log scale. But the drawn ring is pushed outward by packOrbitRadii() to stop orbits colliding, and that push is read back as real distance. Earth reads 1.5 AU; Mars reads ~3.0. Carry the distance from the orbital data instead of reconstructing it from geometry.

These numbers were reproduced independently by running packOrbitRadii(PLANETS) + the log scale against the planet data — they match the issue's table to the digit. Mercury, Venus and Jupiter read right; the rest are inflated by exactly the amount their ring was pushed out.

Root cause

renderOrbit() in src/renderer/bodies.ts labels a ring purely from the geometry it just drew. It solves where the drawn ellipse crosses x = CENTER, then converts each crossing point's pixel distance from the Sun back to AU: 

const auAt = (point) =>
  radiusFromAU(Math.hypot(point.x - CENTER, point.y - CENTER));

The drawn ring is not at its natural log-scale radius. packOrbitRadii() (src/renderer/orbit-packing.ts) sweeps outward from the Sun and pushes each orbit out just enough to keep a minimum gap from its inward neighbour. Earth's push is large because Earth's spacing bubble has to enclose the whole Moon orbit (size 10 + MOON_PIXEL_OFFSET 22 + MOON.size 5 = 37px, vs 6–9px for the other inner planets). The push is cumulative down the outward sweep — Mercury → Earth (+30px) → Mars (+51px, worst) — then resets at any planet whose natural radius already clears its neighbour (Venus, Jupiter) and re-accumulates across Saturn → Uranus → Neptune. computePlanetVisualEllipse() adds that offset uniformly to perihelion and aphelion, so it lands in aPx/bPx too.

renderOrbit() never receives planet.au, planet.eccentricity or the packing offset, so every pushed ring is read back as extra distance. The ring position, the planet marker and the Moon orbit are all fine — only the label text is wrong. Comet orbits are unaffected: renderCometOrbit() is a fully independent function that draws no AU labels and gets no packing offset.

The essence

The orbit ring is a pure function of static data. Its shape comes from planet.au, planet.eccentricity and planet.longitudeOfPerihelion in src/astronomy/planet-data.ts — none of which change with the date. What moves per frame is the planet marker (calculatePlanetOrbit(planet, date) gives its true anomaly on the fixed ring). The ring itself is identical on every render, forever, until someone edits the data or the scale constants.

So the two label distances per planet are constants. And we already know them: at the point where the ellipse is built we are holding au and e. The heliocentric distance anywhere on a Kepler orbit is r = a (1 − e cos E), with E the eccentric anomaly. The vertical-axis crossing solver already computes E as its phi ± delta parameter (for an a cos t / b sin t ellipse, that parameter is the eccentric anomaly). Evaluate au (1 − e cos E) in AU and the pixel scale — and therefore the packing offset — never enters.

—— the drawn ring (packed, rotated by longitudeOfPerihelion)  ·  labels sit at the two axis crossings  ·  each value is the true heliocentric distance at that crossing, computed from au and e — not from the ring's pixel radius

The change

  1. New src/renderer/orbit-labels.ts. At module load, for each planet: build the natural ellipse (ellipseFromApsides(auToRadius(au·(1−e)), auToRadius(au·(1+e))), rotation longitudeOfPerihelion), solve the two vertical-axis crossing eccentric anomalies E = phi ± delta through the shared transform + crossing solver, evaluate au (1 − e cos E) at each, keep the sorted pair. Export ORBIT_LABEL_AU: Record<string, { minAU: number; maxAU: number }>. View- and date-independent; ~16 Math.cos calls, once, at import.
  2. verticalAxisIntersections() in bodies.ts returns each crossing's eccentric anomaly E alongside its { x, y }, so the label module and renderOrbit share one crossing solver rather than each re-deriving the mirror/geometry by hand (the #94 “one mirror” rule).
  3. renderOrbit() gains a { minAU, maxAU } argument and drops radiusFromAU(Math.hypot(…)) entirely. It assigns minAU to whichever drawn crossing is nearer CENTER and maxAU to the farther, then prints "<x.x> AU". A uniform radial packing offset can't flip which side is nearer the Sun, so “nearer drawn crossing ↔ smaller distance” always holds — that is what puts the two values on opposite sides of the ring by construction.
  4. renderSolarSystem() (src/renderer/index.ts) passes ORBIT_LABEL_AU[planet.name] into each renderOrbit call.

radiusFromAU() keeps its other callers (none in renderOrbit after this). Nothing about ring geometry, markers, the Moon orbit, comets, or the season overlay moves.

Approach — designed twice

A. Stay in pixels — subtract the packing offset before inverting

Thread the already-computed packedOffset (index.ts:80) into renderOrbit; label text becomes radiusFromAU(hypot(point − CENTER) − packedOffset). The offset was added uniformly to both apsides, so subtracting it uniformly is exact. One new param, one subtraction; geometry still drives value and placement.

Why not: it keeps a physical quantity round-tripping through a layout-space inverse scale — the exact shape of the bug. The offset still perturbs the printed value through the eccentric anomaly at the crossing (a few 0.01 AU), and renderOrbit stays coupled to the packing table. It patches the leak instead of removing the pipe.

B. Carry the distance from the data ← chosen

Compute au (1 − e cos E) from planet.au / planet.eccentricity, once, in a dedicated module; hand renderOrbit two finished numbers. The pixel scale never touches the label.

Sub-choice inside B — which ellipse feeds the E solve:

  • Packed (drawn) ellipse. Use the same aPx/bPx/cPx the renderer draws. E shifts a hair when packOrbitRadii fattens a ring. Keeps one geometry, but lets the visualization hack back into the number.
  • Natural ellipse. Chosen. Solve E from the orbit's real shape, no packing offset. The label is pure orbital mechanics. Both go through the identical verticalAxisIntersections(rx, ry, orbitTransformComponents(…)) — the natural variant just passes the unpacked aPx/bPx/cPx, no extra hand-rolled mirror path. As a bonus, orbit-labels.ts depends only on PLANETS + svg-utils, never on orbit-packing.ts.
B, natural ellipse. The AU label is a statement about the solar system, not about the drawing. Placement stays on the drawn ring; the number comes from the data that defines the orbit. That removes the contamination class entirely rather than cancelling one instance of it, and it decouples the label from the packing machinery it never should have been reading.

Adjacent opportunities

  • Not folded in. The vertical-axis crossing is not the perihelion/aphelion point, so the two labels are the distances where the ring meets the axis, not the orbit's true min/max. For near-circular orbits the difference is < 0.05 AU and the current design already labels the crossings; showing explicit perihelion/aphelion instead is a product decision, its own issue if wanted.
  • Not folded in. #203 (realistic-distance orbit mode) would make packOrbitRadii's offset zero or near-zero, at which point A and B converge numerically. B is still the right shape there — the label should come from data regardless of how the ring is scaled — so this change is a clean head start, not foreclosed work.
  • Enables. A per-planet AU table computed once at module load is a natural home for any future static orbit-derived display value (orbital period, synodic period) without re-threading data through the renderer.

Design invariant preserved

“Each orbit displays AU distance from Sun” (CLAUDE.md) — still two labels per ring, same position next to the season-divider axis, same 9px sans-serif in ORBIT_COLOR, same one-decimal format. Only the numbers change: they become correct. Ring geometry, the log-scale spacing, the anti-crowding pack, the visibility cone at Earth's orbit, and the back/forward date buttons are all untouched.

LESSONS.md. No prior entry for this class — a derived display quantity reconstructed from post-layout pixels instead of carried from source data. If the build holds the shape, explain-it compound logs it after the slice: physical quantities travel with the data; a layout transform is one-way — never invert it to recover a number you already had.
